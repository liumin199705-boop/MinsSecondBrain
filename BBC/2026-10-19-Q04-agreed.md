# Q04 — agreed 19 October 2026

## Refined question

The BBC is considering a patent-pool licence for a technology it plans to use. How would you assess whether the licence provides suitable coverage and value, and what risks might remain?

## Agreed answer

I would assess the pool licence against the BBC’s actual use.

First, I would establish the technology, standard and version involved, the intended use and territories. I would check whether the licence covers the relevant patents, BBC entities, products, services and activities, including any exclusions.

Second, I would identify gaps and overlap. Some relevant patent owners may remain outside the pool, requiring additional licences. I would also check existing supplier licences and other arrangements to avoid paying twice for the same rights.

Third, I would assess how much of the package the BBC actually needs. The pool may include patents covering features or territories the BBC does not use. I would explore narrower coverage or direct licensing where available. A package containing unused patents is not automatically poor value: I would compare its overall cost, useful coverage and administrative benefits with the alternatives.

I would also review the royalty calculation, reporting and audit obligations, portfolio changes and termination provisions.

Finally, with the senior patent lawyer, I would recommend whether the licence offers suitable coverage and value, explain any remaining exposure and propose how to manage it, including any risk to service continuity.
