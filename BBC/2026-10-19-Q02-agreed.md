# Q02 — agreed 19 October 2026

Question: Our supplier says its patent licence covers us. Is that enough?

## Agreed answer

I would not rely on the supplier’s assurance alone. I would review the relevant licence terms and check whether they cover the patents, BBC entities, technology, territories and activities involved.

I would check how that coverage extends to the BBC, whether through rights covering customers, an authorised sublicence or applicable patent exhaustion rules. I would identify any restrictions or conditions that could leave a gap.

I would distinguish licence coverage from an indemnity. An indemnity may provide financial protection if a claim arises, but does not itself give the BBC permission to use the patented technology or ensure that its service can continue.

I would therefore also check whether the supplier must secure the necessary rights, or modify or replace the affected technology if its use is challenged. I would assess whether those solutions preserve the functionality the BBC needs, how quickly they could be delivered and what disruption could arise in the meantime.

If coverage or protection remained unclear, I would work with the senior patent lawyer to resolve the gaps and obtain any necessary confirmation, additional rights or contractual protection before recommending that the BBC rely on the supplier’s assurance.
