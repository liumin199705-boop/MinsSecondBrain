## Approved model Actions

### 1. Understand the pressures behind each person’s position
I arranged individual conversations with US counsel, the engineering lead and the search teams. Beyond asking what they needed, I explored what made collaboration difficult and what they needed from me to work effectively. I then checked my understanding with them and agreed practical changes.

### 2. Earn credibility by solving a problem for each team
Engineers were receiving overlapping requests, so I consolidated them and clarified priorities with counsel. Search specialists needed better technical context, so I arranged access to the relevant engineer. Counsel needed dependable responses, so I agreed realistic delivery dates with named owners.

I followed those commitments through. This gave each group an early experience of how working with me helped them.

### 3. Create space for honest disagreement
I invited colleagues to flag uncertainty or challenge my interpretation, including privately before wider discussions. When an engineer corrected my understanding, I acknowledged the correction and changed the summary.

I also made my own uncertainties visible and sought specialist input. Over repeated exchanges, that demonstrated that raising a concern would lead to investigation and support.

### 4. Work through friction fairly
When an urgent legal request conflicted with engineering capacity, I spoke with both sides to understand the consequences. I brought them together around the decision that needed supporting and helped agree a staged response: essential information first, with further detail to follow.

I took responsibility for clarifying an ambiguous brief rather than allowing frustration to become personal. That helped preserve confidence on both sides when the work was difficult.

### 5. Build connections that worked beyond me
I introduced the relevant engineers and search specialists directly, explained what each could contribute and facilitated their initial discussions. I encouraged them to explore alternative technical explanations, then brought their findings back to counsel for legal assessment.

I wanted people to understand and trust one another’s expertise, so useful exchanges could continue without every question passing through me.

### 6. Reinforce the relationships through follow-through
I returned to contributors to explain how their input had changed the analysis, acknowledged their contribution in team updates and asked what I could improve in the next round.

I acted on that feedback and maintained contact between urgent requests. Over time, colleagues involved me earlier and approached one another more readily, allowing us to identify gaps and resolve questions before they became deadline problems.

## Recall cue
Start small, follow through, and keep following through. Show that working with you gets results. Create space for disagreement and close the loop.
