# Q06 — agreed 19 October 2026

## Refined question

A patent owner relies on licences with other companies to justify its proposed royalty. How would you assess whether those licences are genuinely comparable to the BBC’s situation?

## Answer for later batch amendments

I would treat those licences as evidence to examine, rather than assume their rates apply directly to the BBC.

First, I would seek sufficient information about the agreements, under appropriate confidentiality arrangements. I would check the patents and standards covered, territories, duration and licensed activities. A licence covering device manufacture may provide a different comparison from one covering a streaming service.

Second, I would check how the stated royalty translates into the amount actually payable under the whole agreement. Relevant terms include the royalty base, volumes, discounts, caps, lump-sum payments and any settlement of past infringement. I would also check for cross-licensing or other rights exchanged, because the cash payment may represent only part of the consideration.

Third, with the senior patent lawyer and economic input where needed, I would put the figures on a consistent basis and assess which differences justify adjustments. I would avoid assigning precise adjustments where the evidence does not support them. I would assess whether differences in treatment are explained by relevant differences in scope and commercial terms, rather than assume that FRAND requires identical rates for every licensee.

Finally, I would explain which agreements provide the strongest comparisons, the limitations of the evidence and the range they reasonably support. I would use that analysis to develop a reasoned negotiating position. The fact that another company accepted a rate would not, by itself, establish that the proposed terms are FRAND for the BBC.
