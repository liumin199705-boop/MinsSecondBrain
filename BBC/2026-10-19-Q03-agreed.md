# Q03 — replacement agreed 19 October 2026

## Question

A patent owner alleges that technology used by the BBC infringes its standard-essential patents. It says its proposed licence is FRAND, but the BBC considers the royalty excessive. How would you assess the demand and advise on the next steps?

## Agreed answer

I would separate two questions: does the BBC need a licence, and are the proposed terms FRAND? I would not accept either the owner’s description of its offer or the BBC’s view that it is excessive without examining the evidence.

First, with engineers and the senior patent lawyer, I would identify the relevant standard, the BBC’s implementation and the asserted patents. I would assess essentiality, validity and infringement, check the relevant FRAND commitment, and establish whether existing supplier or pool licences already cover the use.

Second, I would ask the owner to explain its royalty calculation and provide supporting information, including comparable licences where available under appropriate confidentiality arrangements. I would examine whether those licences are genuinely comparable, considering their portfolio coverage, territories, products, volumes and any cross-licensing. I would also test the royalty base and the other proposed terms, rather than judging the headline rate alone.

I would use that analysis to develop a reasoned negotiating position and, where appropriate, a supported counteroffer. I would engage promptly and constructively, while preserving the BBC’s ability to challenge the patents and proposed terms.

Finally, I would assess the potential cost and service disruption if the dispute escalated. I would recommend a negotiation strategy, timetable and contingency plan, and consider mediation or court determination with senior counsel if agreement could not be reached. The aim would be appropriate rights on defensible terms while managing the risk to the BBC’s services.
