Question: A letter says iPlayer infringes a video-compression patent. What do you do first?

## Agreed answer

I would first check the response deadline and whether the letter threatens proceedings or urgent action. I would preserve the letter and relevant records, and promptly involve the responsible senior lawyer.

I would then clarify the allegation: which patents and territories are involved, which claims are being asserted, and which part of iPlayer is said to infringe. I would check the patent’s status and the sender’s entitlement to assert it.

Working with engineers and patent specialists, I would establish how the relevant technology operates and assess infringement and validity separately. In parallel, I would check existing licences, supplier indemnities and any notification requirements, including under relevant insurance.

If needed, I would coordinate a holding response without admitting liability, request further particulars or more time, and confirm whether any extension had been agreed.

I would give the service owner an initial assessment based on the available facts, highlighting any credible risk to service continuity, the information still needed and a clear timetable for substantive advice.
