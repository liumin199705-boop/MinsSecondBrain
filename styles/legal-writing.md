# Legal writing profile
Status: initial framework; detailed personal style awaits Min's examples and feedback.
Updated: 15 November 2026.

## Established general preferences
Use natural, precise, concise professional English. Remove unnecessary words and clunky phrasing.
Never invent facts, experience, responsibilities or outcomes. Distinguish verified facts, assumptions, proposals and hypothetical examples.
Explain unfamiliar concepts concretely. Match detail to the audience and practical decision.
Use clear short headings and numbered structure when helpful. Times New Roman is an established preference for preparation documents, not an automatic rule for every email or document.

## Proposed legal-advice format — not yet a confirmed personal style
Where suitable, begin with the answer or recommendation, then material facts and assumptions, relevant legal analysis, options and trade-offs, and practical next steps.
Distinguish legal requirements from commercial recommendations and unresolved questions. State jurisdiction and relevant dates when material. Verify current law through authoritative sources.
Adapt structure to the task; do not force a full advice format onto a short email, clause or pleading.

## To calibrate
Preferred examples; intended readers; directness and formality; paragraph length; use of headings and numbering; citation style; treatment of qualifications and risk; preferred terminology; disliked phrases.
